Team Debrief

For team leaders. Paste a meeting transcript and your team charter, and optionally your Breakout Learning results. You get a team-level picture of how you work together: who gets airtime, whether you follow your own charter, which roles fall on one person, and three changes to make next meeting.

How to use this with your own team (3 minutes)
  1. Record a team meeting in Zoom, Teams, or Otter with transcription turned on. Export the transcript (.txt or .vtt). Speaker names must be in it, e.g. Avery Chen: text, [00:01:05] Avery: text, or a WebVTT file.
  2. Paste or upload the transcript in box 1. Everything else is optional, but each extra source makes the debrief sharper.
  3. Paste your team charter in box 2. The tool checks the meeting against your own norms (decision rules, roles, accountability).
  4. Add Breakout Learning data (optional). In box 3, type each member's talk time from their "Session Results" page, one per line: Name, minutes. In box 4, paste the evaluation points from each member's report. Ask teammates to share theirs with you first.
  5. Add individual assessments (optional) in box 5, e.g. conflict styles or personality results.
  6. Click Analyze participation for instant metrics (computed in your browser, nothing is sent). Then click Generate AI debrief for the full analysis.
  7. Share carefully. The team-level sections are meant for the whole team. The private nudges are meant to go to each person individually.

Privacy: Participation metrics are computed in your browser. The AI debrief sends your inputs to an AI model provider's API (OpenAI) to generate the report. Nothing is stored by this site. Get your teammates' consent before uploading their data. Want to see it first? Click Load sample team (a fictional team).

Airtime balance

Share of words spoken in the meeting. The dashed line marks an equal share. We flag anyone under 0.6× or over 1.4× an equal share. A single "balanced / unbalanced" score can hide a quiet member, so this checks each person.

Airtime across the meeting

Each person's share of words in each quarter of the meeting. Look for anyone who fades out or only speaks when called on.

Who responds to whom

How often the speaker in each row was followed by the speaker in each column. One busy row and column means a hub-and-spoke pattern. A spread-out grid means members talk to each other.
